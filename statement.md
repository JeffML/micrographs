# Artist's Statement

_The subjects are familiar — a flower, a grain of pollen, a mineral crystal. The details are not. At a hundred times magnification, the ordinary becomes unrecognizable, and that is where the work begins._

## About the Work

These photographs begin in science — a microscope, a slide, a subject too small to see in detail — but they do not end there. The instrument is a tool, not an authority. What matters is what emerges when light bends through a lens in just the right way: color, form, texture, the uncanny geometry of the natural world at a scale we rarely witness.

This is not documentary work. No claims are made about biology or chemistry. The goal is aesthetic: to find beauty in the overlooked, and to present it in a way that rewards looking.

**On artificial intelligence:** AI plays a limited role in this work — occasionally assisting with background generation or compositional refinement — but the eye behind the lens, the choices of subject and light, and the judgment of when an image is finished remain entirely human.

**On representation:** Some images are faithful renderings of what the microscope sees. Others have been pushed further — color deepened, backgrounds invented, elements composed rather than simply captured. Where an image has crossed from documentation into interpretation, that is intentional and duly noted. The line between the found and the made has always been part of photography's conversation with itself.

## About the Artist

I’m Jeff Lowery, and I’ve always been drawn to projects that require looking a little closer. After spending 35 years in software development, I now divide my time between writing, open-source chess projects, and exploring the natural world. My current fascination is micro-photography. Using specialized lighting and high magnification (40× to 100×), I love finding the brilliant colors and striking abstract patterns hidden in everyday objects. For me, it’s a quiet reminder that there is always more to the world than meets the eye.
